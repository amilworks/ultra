Mono colour — JetBrains Mono against Inter at 430

When body moved 400 → 430, every mono surface that inherited body weight silently jumped a full grade. JetBrains Mono ships as static faces (300/400/500/600), and CSS font-matching resolves a target between 400 and 500 by scanning upward first — so an inherited 430 loads Mono 500. The proof was in document.fonts: only the 500 face was fetched; 400 sat unloaded. Twenty rules inherited; none chose 500.

The two measures pull in opposite directions, and that is the finding. Stroke ratio says 500 is far too black: +14% against the prose it sits inside, and in absolute pixels its stem (1.43px) equals full-size prose (1.41px) on a letter 11% shorter. Texture says mono is lighter either way, because the fixed 0.6em advance spends more of every em on air. Strokes are what make a chip look bold; texture is what makes a paragraph look grey. A chip is read as letterforms, not as a paragraph — so stroke parity is the criterion that matches how chips are seen, and the tint background exists precisely to carry the register shift that texture lightness would otherwise cost.

The parity solve lands at ≈426 — between the static faces. 400 misses by −0.0085; 500 by +0.0243. 400 is 2.9× closer, it is the grade every code editor ships, and Night bloom only pushes rendered weight further up from there. Hence --font-weight-mono: 400, pinned at all 20 inheriting rules, with the contract refusing any mono rule that inherits again.

What was deliberately left alone

Block leading stays 1.2. It is authored (leading-[1.2] in code-block.tsx), it is the JetBrains IDE default that this face was drawn for — the tall x-height and generous internal metrics exist so tight leading still reads — and at 13px the ink pitch (13.4px) clears the 15.6px box with no collisions. GitHub's 1.5 optimises skimming unfamiliar code; Ultra's blocks are output read linearly once. Different job, different number.

Inline size stays 0.88em. X-height parity with prose would want 0.99em (0.5444 / 0.5500), but mono at full size reads oversized — the wide fixed advance does the inflating. 0.88em keeps chips subordinate to the prose line, and the measured chip contrast holds at 6.7:1 on Night's tint, 5.1:1 on Day's — both clear of AA at chip size.

The nine already-pinned mono rules keep their weights. Viewer cues at 500/600 and admin labels at 600 were explicit choices at authoring time — micro-type earning its weight — not rounding accidents.
