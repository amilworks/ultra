Graphik fits worst of the three (3.46%) — not because it is far away on average, but because it has a width signature Inter's axis cannot reproduce: a narrow s (−7.5% against the fit), a wide t (+9.5%) and wide round capitals (O +6.4%). Scaling one axis moves every glyph together; it cannot make s narrow and t wide at once.

All three references land within 1% of each other on x-height relative to cap (0.724–0.732), and all three sit well below Inter at text sizes (0.750). That single number is most of why Inter reads as a UI typeface and these read as brand typefaces — Inter's x-height is deliberately taller so small text holds up on screen. Inter's own axis crosses the references' consensus at about opsz 24. Everything else here is width; this is proportion.
