Matching Söhne's colour with Inter's weight axis

Söhne looks darker than Inter at the same size because its strokes are thicker relative to its letters — stem/x-height 0.1721 against Inter's 0.1609, a 6.9% difference. Inter's absolute stem is only 2.7% thinner; the gap is that Inter's x-height is larger, so the same stroke covers proportionally less. Raising Inter's weight axis closes it. Two independent methods agree on wght 430.

This page sets no -webkit-font-smoothing, matching Ultra's app CSS, so it renders type the way the product does. The other font-lab pages set antialiased; on a Retina Mac that turns out to make no visible difference (last section), so the pages are comparable either way.

Geometric — solving stem/x-height from the outlines — lands at wght 431. Photometric — rasterising a real sentence and integrating ink over the x-height band — lands at wght 430. They were computed independently and agree to within one axis unit. Note that Graphik is the lightest of the three by a wide margin, which is why it reads as airy next to Söhne.

A caution on the earlier number: measuring ink per unit of line length shows Inter 400 and Söhne as identical (0.88 each). That metric is misleading — Söhne is narrower, so it packs equal ink into a shorter line. Perception follows ink per unit of letter area, because you read letters, not line-inches. That is the column above.

The second line is how every other font-lab page renders Inter. On this display the two are indistinguishable, and that is the expected result on any Retina Mac: macOS uses grayscale antialiasing at HiDPI regardless, so -webkit-font-smoothing: antialiased is close to a no-op. It mattered on non-Retina screens. So this was not a hidden weight lever — the gap you are seeing is the typeface, and wght is the honest way to close it.

Knock-on: the weight ladder

Ultra's scale in styles.css is body 400, nav/data/action 500, label/heading 600, strong 700. Moving body to 430 leaves only 70 units between body and nav instead of 100, which flattens a distinction the calm hierarchy leans on. If body moves, the ladder probably should too — something like 430 / 530 / 620 / 700 keeps the steps roughly even. That is a deliberate change to --font-weight-*, not a side effect worth absorbing silently.
